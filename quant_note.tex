{\large\bfseries Covered Calls Around M\&A Agreement Disclosures}\par
Solo submission \hfill GQH Systematic Trading / Massive 8-K Options\par

\textbf{Hypothesis and mechanism.}
Writing an out-of-the-money call may add more net value to synthetic stock after M\&A agreement filings than on ordinary dates for the same names:
\[
\theta=\mathbb{E}[(CC-S)_{\rm net}\mid\text{event}]
       -\mathbb{E}[(CC-S)_{\rm net}\mid\text{ordinary}]>0.
\]
Strategic-upside demand could support premiums [1]. Genuine deal risk and repricing before entry compete with this explanation. Covered calls contain equity, volatility and timing exposures [2]. The literature motivates the test; it does not establish an M\&A premium or stock rally.

\textbf{Method and provenance.}
V4 uses static TOP100 and Massive's \texttt{acquisition\_agreement} or \texttt{merger\_agreement} tags, deduplicated by accession; ambiguous filers are skipped. Tags do not prove buyer status, control, first announcement or unique transactions. The preceding-filing-session chain selects 90--180 DTE, nearest 120, approximately 5\% OTM. Entry is the next NYSE session close; exit is entry plus 21 sessions, capped at expiry. Synthetic stock is ATM call minus put plus strike discounted at flat 4\% carry.

The screened-buyer hypothesis was committed as \texttt{63664eb} before first IS. V4 changes selection/timing \emph{after earlier IS inspection}; it is not the original preregistration. IS is 2024--2025; January--August 2026 OOS was evaluated once with unchanged source. Judges alone evaluate sealed dates. Historical hypotheses and V1--V4 runs remain disclosed.

Each option leg costs 5\% of premium per side; double costs are a fixed stress. Up to 120 seed-7 ordinary draws lie over 30 calendar days from agreement tags. No price-based redrawing. Missing or over-three-session-stale marks remain unavailable.

\textbf{Fixed primary result.}
IS $\theta=0.87$ pp, exploratory 95\% CI $[-0.37,2.17]$; OOS $3.38$ pp, CI $[1.18,5.85]$. IS has 56 accessions/25 issuers and 38 finite increments; OOS 25/14 and 15 finite increments. OOS matched stock/covered-call means are $-4.50/-3.38\%$ at events versus $1.79/-0.48\%$ on ordinary dates. Positive $\theta$ is \emph{not profitable covered-call performance}: short calls cushion falling event stocks while limiting rising controls. It does not establish mispricing or causality. Table units are percentage points; counts are finite event/ordinary observations.

\textbf{Robustness and interpretation.}
Doubled-cost $\theta$: IS 0.95 pp (CI $[-0.39,2.34]$), OOS 3.51 pp ($[1.21,6.07]$). All 27 close-entry/21-session expiry--OTM--tag point estimates are positive in each window; all 972 correlated cells per window are retained, without choosing a winner. Pre/filing-day views are diagnostic. OOS all-issuer leave-one-out estimates range 2.08--4.15 pp. Row bootstrap ignores overlapping deals/issuers: OOS effective issuer counts are 9.8 events/7.0 controls. Evidence supports the relative increment in this proxy sample, not a stable profitable edge. No OOS retuning.

\textbf{Capital, risk and trading.}
Each window starts at \$1 million with no carried positions: one contract per signal, one concurrent per name, zero cash return, no leverage. Stock-reference name/industry/total limits are 10/25/100\%; pause/resume drawdown 10/5\%, using prior-session state. The inherited 14-name industry map makes this a different sample from the event study. IS has one unpriced entry and one unresolved exit; OOS has two open positions at the window end.
